Google Drive keeps your files in one place. You can open them from a computer, Chromebook, phone, or tablet, create new files, keep them organized, and share them with others.<sup class="guide-fn"><a href="#source-1">1</a></sup>

This guide covers the basics: how Drive is laid out, what you need in a browser, and how to work offline. For storage limits and freeing space, see [Manage Google storage](/guide/manage-google-storage).

## Computer (including Chromebook)

Open [drive.google.com](https://drive.google.com) in your browser.<sup class="guide-fn"><a href="#source-1">1</a></sup>

### Create or upload with New

1. At the top left, click **New**.
2. Create or upload a file or folder.<sup class="guide-fn"><a href="#source-1">1</a></sup>

### Home and My Drive

In **Home**, you can open files you or others recently opened, shared, or edited, and use the search bar and filter chips (Type, People, Modified date, Location).<sup class="guide-fn"><a href="#source-1">1</a></sup>

**My Drive** shows files and folders you upload or sync, plus Google Docs, Sheets, Slides, and Forms you create.<sup class="guide-fn"><a href="#source-1">1</a></sup>

Tip: You can set **Home** or **My Drive** as your start page under **Settings** → **Settings** → **Start page**.<sup class="guide-fn"><a href="#source-1">1</a></sup>

### Common actions

- **File details** (activity, sharing, recent changes): select a file, then **View details**.<sup class="guide-fn"><a href="#source-1">1</a></sup>
- **Share**: select a file or folder, then **Share** under the search bar (viewer, commenter, or editor).<sup class="guide-fn"><a href="#source-1">1</a></sup>
- **Delete**: select a file or folder, then **Move to trash**.<sup class="guide-fn"><a href="#source-1">1</a></sup>
- **Star**: right-click a file or folder → **Organize** → **Add to starred**.<sup class="guide-fn"><a href="#source-1">1</a></sup>
- **More actions** (rename, share, move, download): select a file or folder, then **More actions** under the search bar.<sup class="guide-fn"><a href="#source-1">1</a></sup>

## Android

Open the **Google Drive** app.<sup class="guide-fn"><a href="#source-2">2</a></sup>

- **Home**: files you are likely to use (**Suggested**) or recent changes (**Activity**).<sup class="guide-fn"><a href="#source-2">2</a></sup>
- **Starred**: next to a file, tap **More actions** → **Add to starred**.<sup class="guide-fn"><a href="#source-2">2</a></sup>
- **Shared**: files others shared with you and files you shared; use **More actions** → **Share** to share.<sup class="guide-fn"><a href="#source-2">2</a></sup>
- **Files**: all your files; switch between **My Drive** and **Computer** (items synced with Drive for desktop).<sup class="guide-fn"><a href="#source-2">2</a></sup>

## iPhone and iPad

Open the **Google Drive** app.<sup class="guide-fn"><a href="#source-3">3</a></sup>

The layout matches Android: **Home** (Suggested / Activity), **Starred**, **Shared**, and **Files** (My Drive / Computer).<sup class="guide-fn"><a href="#source-3">3</a></sup>

- To star or share: next to a file, tap **More actions**, then **Add to starred** or **Share**.<sup class="guide-fn"><a href="#source-3">3</a></sup>
- To switch accounts: tap your profile picture at the top right and pick another account. Files stay separate per account.<sup class="guide-fn"><a href="#source-3">3</a></sup>

## Browser and system requirements

For the best experience with Drive, Docs, Sheets, Slides, Vids, and Forms, use one of the **two most recent versions** of a supported browser, and turn on **cookies** and **JavaScript**.<sup class="guide-fn"><a href="#source-4">4</a></sup>

Other browsers may work, but some features might be missing.<sup class="guide-fn"><a href="#source-4">4</a></sup>

Drive for desktop (Windows / Mac app) is separate from the website. Google lists supported Windows and macOS versions on that help page; Linux uses Drive on the web instead.<sup class="guide-fn"><a href="#source-4">4</a></sup>

## Use files offline (computer)

Without internet, you can still view and edit **Google Docs**, **Sheets**, and **Slides** if you set offline access up first.<sup class="guide-fn"><a href="#source-5">5</a></sup>

Before you turn it on:

- Stay connected to the internet for setup.
- Use **Google Chrome** or **Microsoft Edge** (not private browsing).
- Install and turn on the **Google Docs Offline** Chrome extension.
- Make sure the device has enough free space.<sup class="guide-fn"><a href="#source-5">5</a></sup>

### Turn on offline access

1. Open Chrome or Edge (on Chrome, sign into the account you want).
2. Go to [drive.google.com/drive/settings](https://drive.google.com/drive/settings).
3. Check **Create, open, and edit your recent Google Docs, Sheets, and Slides files on this device while offline**.<sup class="guide-fn"><a href="#source-5">5</a></sup>

### Make a file available offline

1. Go to [drive.google.com](https://drive.google.com).
2. Right-click a Docs, Sheets, or Slides file.
3. Click **Make available offline**.<sup class="guide-fn"><a href="#source-5">5</a></sup>

To select several files, hold **Shift** or **Ctrl** (Windows) / **Command** (Mac) while clicking.<sup class="guide-fn"><a href="#source-5">5</a></sup>

To preview what is ready offline: click **Ready for offline** (top right) → **Offline preview**.<sup class="guide-fn"><a href="#source-5">5</a></sup>

Edits you make offline sync when you reconnect; new changes overwrite earlier offline changes, and you can review them in version history.<sup class="guide-fn"><a href="#source-5">5</a></sup>

If you use **Drive for desktop**, mirrored files stay available offline; streamed files can be marked available offline from File Explorer (Windows) or Finder (Mac). Docs, Sheets, and Slides still need the web offline setting above.<sup class="guide-fn"><a href="#source-5">5</a></sup>

## Chromebook offline

Even without internet, a Chromebook can still view, create, and edit documents, spreadsheets, or slides using Google Drive (after offline is set up as above).<sup class="guide-fn"><a href="#source-8">8</a></sup>

Important: some offline apps and services do not work in Incognito or guest mode.<sup class="guide-fn"><a href="#source-8">8</a></sup>

Google’s Chromebook offline article also covers Gmail offline, Play Movies downloads, offline games, and Google Keep notes. For school file work, focus on Drive offline settings first.<sup class="guide-fn"><a href="#source-8">8</a></sup>

## Use files offline (Android)

Without Wi‑Fi or mobile data, you can still view and edit Docs, Sheets, and Slides that you saved offline.<sup class="guide-fn"><a href="#source-6">6</a></sup>

1. Open the **Google Drive** app.
2. Next to the file, tap **More**.
3. Tap **Make available offline**.<sup class="guide-fn"><a href="#source-6">6</a></sup>

To find offline files: open Drive, Docs, Sheets, or Slides → tap **Menu** → **Offline**.<sup class="guide-fn"><a href="#source-6">6</a></sup>

## Use files offline (iPhone and iPad)

Without Wi‑Fi or mobile data, you can still view and edit Docs, Sheets, and Slides that you prepared for offline use.<sup class="guide-fn"><a href="#source-7">7</a></sup>

1. Open the **Google Drive** app.
2. Next to the file, tap **More**.
3. Turn on **Available offline** (saves a preview offline).<sup class="guide-fn"><a href="#source-7">7</a></sup>

Tip: To edit a Docs, Sheets, or Slides file offline, use the Docs, Sheets, or Slides app.<sup class="guide-fn"><a href="#source-7">7</a></sup>

To find offline files: open Drive, Docs, Sheets, or Slides → tap **Menu** → **Offline**.<sup class="guide-fn"><a href="#source-7">7</a></sup>
